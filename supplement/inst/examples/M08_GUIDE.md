# M08: repeated testing and change in R

This companion follows all six chapter units with selected runnable teaching calculations. It is not an implementation of every advanced integration or a clinical scoring tool. All records, priors and thresholds are synthetic. The chapter and code use the same specified inputs and assumptions.

## Run the examples

Install/restore the locked environment using the companion README if needed. From the source root, run `Rscript --vanilla supplement/inst/examples/m08-change.R`; from another working directory use the quoted full path to that script. Quote the full path when it contains spaces. The script finds its own inputs, creates `outputs/m08-change/`, and writes 20 CSV tables plus provenance and session information. It uses base R, tibble, dplyr, and readr. Setup is described in [the companion reproduction guide](../../REPRODUCIBILITY.md).

For R beginners: `<-` saves a result under a name; `c()` collects numbers into a vector; `cbind()` makes design columns; `diag()` makes a diagonal matrix; `source()` loads named calculations. Each CSV row is one explicitly labeled assumption or coefficient, not a patient. Open the CSV in a spreadsheet or read it with `readr::read_csv()`. Preserve `Inf` and `-Inf`: these endpoints describe absence of finite information, not missing values.

## Inputs and steps

1. **Joint occasions.** Keep the same-person means 50 and 52, SD 10 and correlation .8. Compute the raw difference, expected retest, conditional innovation and their distinct standardizations. `joint-comparisons.csv` also computes the error probability of the marginal difference rule among peers at each fixed baseline. This is a known-law experiment, not a finite-reference fit.
2. **Fitted reference.** Fit intercept, baseline slope and residual scale from the six scores printed in the chapter. `scalar-reference-coefficients.csv` reports coefficient estimates, confidence intervals and proper posterior marginals. `scalar-retest-prediction.csv` adds both leverage and new-performance variance. Its Student scale is not its SD. `scalar-offset-joint-update.csv` adds the case and offset coefficient to one likelihood and refits all nuisance parameters. Its third row targets an observed conditional mean offset, not the new retest or latent loss.
3. **Practice and states.** `state-sample-covariance.csv` computes sample covariance from all 64 raw four-visit records in `M08_INPUTS/state-records.csv`; they are supplied synthetic observations, not an output fitted elsewhere. `state-finite-confidence.csv` constructs direction-wise chi-square bands with an allocated union-bound error, then conservative whole-identified-set outer bounds for three and four visits. Components are score variances; trait share is dimensionless. `necessarily_empty` flags an impossible outer-bound condition. The function does not perform sharp continuous-region projection. `serial-equivalence-family.csv` uses supplied covariance 13/7/5.5 and displays three observationally equivalent three-visit component choices, whose fourth lag differs. Stable total remains g+s; it does not separate trait from specificity.
4. **Latent change and items.** `gaussian-latent-change.csv` updates both states under the declared correlated prior and variance-9 errors. New-difference variance adds fresh errors while retaining states; a third state’s evolution is not modeled. `conditional-item-confidence.csv` inverts inclusive finite count tails; `item-coordinate-confidence.csv` transforms separately allocated binomial intervals with logit. Their difference rectangle is an alternative enclosure, not the conditional interval. `known-item-posterior-refinement.csv` integrates the SAME counts under three proper state priors at two quadrature orders. `evidence` is an adaptive change integral over a Gaussian-quadrature average; `quadrature_evidence` is an independent two-dimensional sum. Contrast mean, interval endpoints and decline probability are latent units. Expected total difference and probability of a negative new total difference refer to new responses at retained states; neither is a posterior point-null probability.
5. **Trajectories.** `personal-slope-sensitivity.csv` fits intercept, slope and noise scale from the six scores for independent errors, declared AR(.5), or a stronger slope prior. Confidence and credibility columns target the personal observed-score slope in points/year. `mean_variance` is the posterior noise variance mean. The shape is supplied, not learned from these six scores. `curvature-sensitivity.csv` updates endpoint states and integrates an independent uniform midpoint shift with radius M/8. It reports whole-midpoint-set confidence and actual-midpoint credibility; curvature is not learned from endpoints.
6. **Historical baseline and causal targets.** `proxy-independent-prior.csv` updates premorbid level and change under independent original-coordinate priors. `proxy-operational-bias-prior.csv` instead retains an independent bias prior in operational coordinates; variance adds its 0 or 36 variance. This is not the same joint prior as independent P/change/bias. `randomized-average-effect.csv` integrates two learned beta arm probabilities, with full uncertainty. `trial-entire-set-confidence.csv` projects allocated exact arm intervals onto the average and entire benefit set. `unlearned-coupling-refinement.csv` integrates a conditional beta CDF for three unlearned coupling priors, retaining both marginal posteriors. `noncompletion-exact-means.csv` uses full assigned denominators and distinguishes independent missing-outcome priors from within-arm independence. It reports exact means only. `noncompletion-entire-set-confidence.csv` uses four binomial lower endpoints with alpha/4 allocation; within-arm category dependence does not justify multiplying their coverage probabilities.

## Numerical accuracy and boundaries

Formulas and finite sums use ordinary floating-point arithmetic. Adaptive integrals request relative tolerance 1e-9 or 1e-10; this is a numerical request, not a mathematical coverage proof. Known-item average quadrature is increased from 128 to 256 nodes; compare the two rows for each prior. Quantiles use CDF root finding. Gaussian tails outside finite root brackets are handled by the numerical algorithms; no exact decimal theorem is claimed.

Benefit calculations use midpoint probability quadrature at 160 and 320 points per learned marginal. These are approximate sensitivity illustrations: endpoints can differ from the chapter’s more refined values by about .001 at the lower order. Use the refinement CSV to see actual differences; do not replace printed chapter numbers with this coarser result or treat agreement as a certified error bound. No random seed is needed: these algorithms are deterministic.

Input guards reject invalid probabilities, nonpositive covariance, noninteger counts, improper scale-prior inputs and deficient frequentist regression designs. At exactly zero residual scale the confidence interval is the whole real line; the proper prior still supplies a normalized posterior. Infinite count endpoints are retained. Functions normalize proper models rather than assigning posterior mass to a zero-measure null by accident.

Full-precision observations for all chapter examples that use stored records are supplied in [M08_INPUTS](M08_INPUTS/README.md), including the joint anchor/item, matrix-reference, factorial, trajectory, proxy and trial experiments. These are inputs, not precomputed posterior results.

## Coverage map and limits

These selected examples cover all six units;they do not implement every high-dimensional numerical integration in the chapter. The full equations and synthetic inputs remain in the chapter. In particular,this guide does not advertise fresh R reproduction of matrix-normal/IW sensitivity tables,full factorial/alias posteriors,structured state posterior component quantiles,noisy-anchor/item joint and drift posteriors,joint fitted-norm percentile contrasts,unknown proxy precision or continuous-trial posteriors,or full missing-outcome posterior intervals. No stored posterior result is copied into a new CSV as a calculation. The scripts expose the named algorithms above with enough intermediate quantities to check their targets and conditioning.

Chapter mapping:joint-comparisons → joint occasions;scalar reference/prediction/offset → fitted reference;state-confidence/equivalence → practice/forms/states;Gaussian/count → latent change;personal slope/curvature → trajectories;proxy/trial/noncompletion → baseline and causal targets. Table-specific mappings are given in the six numbered steps above. [The chapter source](../../../book/chapters/08-change.md) supplies the complete derivations and advanced integral representations.

## Verification

Run `Rscript supplement/run_checks.R` from the source root for inherited and new tests. New tests compare GLS to direct whitening, enumerate finite count coverage at declared states, check normal and beta identities, preserve floor endpoints and check numerical representations. Separate numerical representations and documented rounding benchmarks are checked in the tests. The source distribution is tested in a separate copy on the recorded host. Tests, numerical refinement and rendering support different claims; none establishes measurement validity or clinical benefit.
